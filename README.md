# Measuring National Identity with Conjoint Experiments Using the Case of Taiwan

**Steven Denney** (Leiden University) · **H. Christoph Steinhardt** (University of Vienna)

## Abstract

How should scholars measure national identity? Conventional survey batteries, such as the International Social Survey Programme's National Identity module, ask respondents to rate identity attributes independently, producing ceiling effects and giving researchers no information about how respondents weigh competing criteria against one another. We propose choice-based conjoint experiments as an alternative. Using original survey data from Taiwan (*n* = 2,050), we field two conjoint experiments: a binary design replicating the ISSP battery, and a multi-level design with nine Taiwan-specific attributes. Across both designs, respondents trade off ascriptive, civic, and voluntarist criteria, a structure that standard batteries cannot register. First, despite intense polarization over the categorical labels Taiwanese citizens choose for themselves, the substantive content respondents endorse is highly consensual: voluntarist and civic markers (feeling Taiwanese, supporting democracy, civic engagement) carry more weight than ascriptive markers across subgroups. Second, voluntarist and civic levels carry enough weight that profiles starting from a "low" ascriptive baseline (non-Han ancestry, Mainland China origin) are still chosen at rates at or above parity when those levels are present. The contest over identity labels in Taiwan masks a wide consensus on the civic and voluntarist content of national membership.

**Keywords**: national identity, conjoint analysis, Taiwan, survey methodology, nationalism
